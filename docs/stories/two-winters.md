# Two Winters

*The band of seed 21, from their first spring to their last. Every event in this story is in the record; the names are for reading, and what each of them felt is what their bodies and their brains recorded, no more. Ash, Esk, Cael and Gant were women; Bren, Dov, Fenn and Hael were men.*

---

## The first spring

There were eight of them, and on the first night they were still strangers to the place. They had come to themselves on a strip of grass between the forest and the water, with the tundra a grey line to the north, and the first thing most of them did was dig. Bren, Cael and Dov found roots in the same patch and pulled them up side by side. Gant stood near Esk and reached out to her, and kept reaching out to her, every half hour through the dark, as if to make sure she was still there.

Cael started the first fight before the night was out. At half past one she tried to kick Bren off his feet and missed; half an hour later she got hold of Ash's foot and held it. Nobody was hurt. Nobody could have said why she did it, and the record cannot either: there was no blow to answer, nothing taken. It was simply what Cael did when she was close to someone and had nothing better in hand. She would go on doing it for two years.

By three in the morning they were asleep in a heap, all but Bren, who dug roots alone until half past three and then lay down too.

They learned the place in a week. The forest ran down to a marsh, and the marsh was water you could drink without walking anywhere: you stood under the trees with the wet at your feet. That was the whole of their fortune, and they never left it. There was honey in the trees as well, and on the second morning Cael went up for it and came down stung. Fenn tried the next afternoon and was stung the same. Cael went back. She would go back eight times.

On the fourth evening Ash and Esk were both parched, not from want of water but from not drinking it, and both learned their lesson and were never parched again for a year and a half. On the sixth day Gant fell sick from the marsh, and on the seventh Dov did, and each got over it in two days. That too was the shape of things to come: the marsh gave them water, and the water gave them the sickness, and they drank anyway, because it was there.

They paired off in the first week, without ceremony. Bren and Cael lay together on the second morning; Esk and Fenn on the fourth. Then Bren, who seems to have liked everyone, began with Esk as well, twice on the tenth day. Gant reached out and reached out and was not, that spring, chosen by anyone.

On the tenth morning something happened that none of them could have planned. Hael, who kept to himself and to Dov, had a habit of leaving food on the ground where he stood. An aurochs had been coming to it. That morning the record says the aurochs was at ease with him: it had eaten what he left so many times that it no longer minded him near. Hael had tamed an animal the way a man leaves crumbs for a bird, without meaning to, and probably without noticing.

## The first summer

Summer was Cael's season. She was stung on the first night, kicked Esk in the neck on the third day and was stung again that afternoon, and again before dawn the next day. On the fourth evening the marsh showed its other face: a crocodile took Dov at the water's edge and tore a quarter of the life out of him in one bite. He went down where he stood. Two and a half hours later he got up.

Cael tried to hold Bren's foot on the fifth morning and he broke the hold; she kicked Fenn in the small hours of the sixth; she butted Bren on the eighth evening and shoved him back a pace. On the eighth night she was stung for the fifth time. None of it stopped her lying with Bren, or him with her.

A heat wave came on the seventh day and sat on them. Bren and Esk lay together through it, six times that summer by the record's count, more than any other pair that year. Ash lay with Fenn once and with Bren once.

And there was the other thing, the quiet thing, that Hael and Dov had between them. On the ninth morning Dov took food that Hael had left on the ground. On the tenth, Hael took food Dov had left, twice. The record calls it sharing. It was two men who stood near each other, each leaving a little of what he had where the other would find it.

## Autumn

The sickness came back with the cold. Ash fell ill from the water on the first afternoon of autumn, and that evening a cold snap set in; by one in the morning Fenn had it from her. Cael got it from the water on the fourth day and gave it to Gant by noon. They were all over it within two or three days, every time, and every time someone else was coming down with it.

They fought more in autumn than in any other season, and the fights began to answer one another, which they had not before. Fenn body-checked Bren in the night of the fifth day and clinched him in the morning; at half past one that afternoon Bren struck back, got Fenn in a clinch of his own, and Fenn broke it. Ash went after Fenn three times across two days, a clinch, a butt, a kick at his head that missed; at half past nine on the seventh morning Fenn turned on her and tried a chokehold, and she broke it. The weather cleared that same hour. Two days later the rains failed.

Esk had taken to the hives. She was stung on the third morning and again on the sixth, and she was carrying Bren's attentions less now; after the summer she and Bren did not lie together again. Bren had gone to Gant, on the second night of autumn, and to Ash on the third. Gant, who had reached out to everyone all spring, was chosen at last.

On the last day of autumn Hael fell sick from the water, and by eleven Dov had it, from the water and from Hael both.

## The first winter

It began mildly. Fenn clinched Cael and grabbed Bren's leg on the first day, and Hael and Dov got over their sickness on the second. On the third morning the crocodile took Dov again, in the same way as in summer, a quarter of him gone in a bite, and he lay in the mud four hours before he could stand. That afternoon Hael fell sick again from the same water. That night Bren struck at an animal, the only time anyone did that year, and we do not know what it was.

Cael and Fenn had found each other in the cold. They lay together on the second day and the fourth; on the fifth, Cael went back to Bren.

The cold snap came at three in the afternoon on the fourth day.

What the snap did was simple. The forest where they camped was the warmest ground for miles, and in ordinary winter it was enough. Under the snap, the air in the forest fell to freezing and below, and then even the trees were not enough: a body under them lost heat faster than it made it unless it was lying still, and even lying still it needed other bodies beside it. The ones who slept in a heap were all right. The ones who stood, or sat alone, were not.

A wolf bit Ash at four in the morning on the fifth day. At eight that evening Esk was freezing in air at minus nine. At half past ten that night Hael, who had wandered up onto the tundra edge where the air was minus twenty-one, was freezing too. He came back down to the water. Ash was freezing at three the next morning at minus fifteen. At six, Hael went down.

He got up again; the record has him drinking at half past ten. For the next day and a half he did almost nothing else. He lay on his bed of leaves at the water's edge, in the forest, and every half hour he rose and drank, and lay down, and rose and drank. He gathered roots three times. He ate four times. He was the loneliest of the eight, three touches a day against the others' dozen, and no one was lying beside him. At three in the morning on the seventh day he was freezing in air at exactly zero, and he drank, and drank, and drank, five times before dawn, and slept an hour, and dug roots at seven, and drank at half past seven and half past eight and nine and half past ten. At half past ten he died, three days into the snap, with the water two steps away and his body full of it.

Esk had stopped eating. From the fifth day she sat at the marsh camp and drank: forty-six times in six days, by the record, and gathered once. By nine on the sixth evening she was starving. She lay down at two in the morning on the eighth day, too weak to stand, and at half past eight she starved to death with the band's food around her and her belly full of water.

Dov was freezing on the seventh afternoon, and again at midnight, and he went down on the eighth morning. Ash went down at noon. Fenn had been freezing in the small hours of the seventh at minus fourteen, and he held. Dov held too, barely: the record says that nearly dying left him haunted, that afternoon, at half past two. He would be downed, on and off, for most of the rest of the winter; he got up for good on the fifth morning of spring, a hundred and sixty-four hours after he last fell.

Ash froze to death at dawn on the ninth day, five days into the snap. That afternoon the record says of Fenn that long hardship had hardened him.

Three of eight. The cold had not killed anyone who slept in the heap. It had killed the one who was alone, the one who would not eat, and the one who would not stop moving.

## The second spring

Five came out of the winter: Bren, Cael, Dov, Fenn, Gant. Dov could not stand until the fifth day. The others carried on exactly as before, because they knew no other way to carry on.

Cael held off a predator in the small hours of the second day, the first time anyone had stood their ground, and three hours later tried to choke Fenn. The rains failed again that afternoon. Bren fell sick from the water on the third night and gave it to Fenn, then Gant, then Cael, one after another through the week, while Cael kept kicking at him and Fenn clinched him back. Fenn held off a predator on the eighth morning. Bren struck at an animal on the ninth. The drought went on.

Bren and Cael lay together on the fifth day. Of the twenty-eight pairings in the record, Bren is in twenty, and two of the women he had lain with were dead. The band's future now ran through one man and two women.

## The second summer

Bren was starving on the first day of summer. The weather cleared on the second, and that afternoon, at two o'clock, Cael conceived. The record is sure of the father. It was the only conception in two years.

Cael held off a predator again the next evening. Then the summer turned on Bren. A wolf bit Dov in the night of the eighth; that morning Bren fell sick; at three in the afternoon the crocodile took him at the water, a quarter of his life in a bite, and he went down. At half past four, down and bleeding, he held it off. At seven he stood. The next morning he fell again, too weak, and Fenn caught the sickness from him, and at half past one that afternoon nearly dying left Bren haunted, as it had Dov. He got over the sickness on the tenth. He did not get over the rest.

In the same week Bren and Gant lay together twice, and Fenn and Gant once. Gant, at the end, had everyone.

## The second autumn

The band died in the second autumn, though two of them walked on into a third year.

A wolf bit Dov at half past four on the first morning and he went down and got up in half an hour. Gant fell sick from the water. On the third morning Dov fell sick, fell over, went down from the wound, and at half past one in the afternoon died of the sickness, the first of the five. Bren held off a predator that night.

On the fifth afternoon the crocodile came for Bren a last time. It barely touched him, two per cent of what he had, but he had almost nothing left, and he was killed in the water at half past two. That evening what remained of Esk, dead since the winter, was bones. Gant took food that Bren had left on the ground before he died.

The rains failed on the seventh. Hael's bones came up on the eighth. Fenn, hardened, haunted by nothing, the strongest of them, was parched at two in the morning on the ninth day, in a drought, beside a marsh; Ash's bones came up at noon; Fenn went down that night and died of thirst at dawn on the tenth, with the water where it had always been.

## The second winter, and after

Two were left: Cael, carrying Bren's child, and Gant. A cold snap set in on the second morning, and half an hour later the crocodile took Cael, a quarter of her, and she went down and three hours later stood up. Gant was freezing at three in the morning on the fifth, sixth and seventh days, in air just under zero, and went down on the seventh. The weather cleared on the eighth.

Cael, in the ninth day of winter, pregnant, alone with one sick companion, went up for honey and was stung, fell sick, and went up again an hour and a half later and was stung again. It was her seventh and eighth time. On the tenth day another snap set in, and Gant caught the sickness from the water and from Cael.

At nine on the first morning of the third spring the crocodile took Cael at the water, and this time she did not get up. She was still carrying the child. At three that afternoon Gant died of the sickness, and that was the end of the band, two years and five days after they had dug their first roots together.

## What the record says about why

They chose their ground well. Forest for the cold, water at their feet. They slept together, made beds of leaves, left food where others would find it, stood their ground against what came for them. Eight people with no fire, no weapons and no hunting lived through two winters and two droughts on roots and nuts and marsh water.

The marsh killed them. Its water gave them the sickness nineteen times and two of them died of it; its crocodile came for them six times and took two more. They never moved. There was other water, a good deal of it, and nothing in the record suggests any of them connected the place with what it did to them.

The cold took the three who could not keep still or keep company. The drought took Fenn because he would not walk to water. Hael died drinking. Esk died drinking. Of all the things they did too much, it was that.

And Bren, who fathered the only child and lay with every woman in the band, was taken by the same crocodile that had been biting them for two years, from two per cent of his strength, at the same water, in the same way. No one learned the crocodile. There were twenty-eight matings and one conception, because the women died first and the man was bitten, sick, and haunted for most of the second year.

That is the whole of it. They did not lack for courage or for company. They lacked the one idea that would have saved them, which was to go somewhere else.
